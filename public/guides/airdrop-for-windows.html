AirDrop for Windows: what actually works

Windows has never had AirDrop, and Microsoft has never built an equivalent. If you've searched for this, you already know that. Here's what to use instead, and why the usual workarounds (emailing yourself a file, a USB cable, Bluetooth) are worse than they need to be.

Last checked 19 August 2026

Why there's no real answer inside Windows itself

Windows has "Nearby Sharing," which does Bluetooth-then-Wi-Fi transfer between two Windows PCs. It doesn't talk to an iPhone or an Android phone at all, so it solves a narrower problem than the one you probably have: getting a file between a phone and a PC.

What to use instead

  1. Open dropitover.com on the Windows PC, and on the phone or other device, in any browser.
  2. If both are on the same Wi-Fi, they show up in each other's "Devices on your Wi-Fi" list within a second or two. Tap the device, pick the file, and accept on the other side. The file goes straight between the two browsers.
  3. If they're not on the same network (the phone is on mobile data, or they're in different places), upload the file instead and share the link it gives you. It gets encrypted, and it stays valid for however long you set, up to 7 days. Open it on the PC whenever you're ready.

Nothing here needs an account, an app install, or a cable. It runs in whatever browser is already open.

Why not just email it to yourself?

It works, but it's slower and it has real limits: most inboxes cap attachments around 25 MB, so a video or a large PDF often won't send at all. A link has no such cap for anything under 2 GB, and it doesn't leave a copy sitting in your inbox forever.

Sending the other way: Windows to phone

The same steps work in reverse. Same Wi-Fi: pick the phone from the device list on the PC. Not on the same network: create a link on the PC, open it on the phone.

Does this work with an iPhone specifically?

Yes. Since everything runs in a normal web browser rather than a platform-specific app, it doesn't matter whether the other device is an iPhone, an Android phone, a Mac, or another Windows machine.
