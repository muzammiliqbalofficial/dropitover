Wi-Fi file transfer: Android to PC

No cable, no Google Drive upload-then-download round trip, no app to install on either side. If the phone and the PC are on the same Wi-Fi, this takes about ten seconds.

Last checked 19 August 2026

What you need

  • The Android phone and the PC connected to the same Wi-Fi network. This is the part that actually matters; the transfer itself needs no setup beyond it.
  • A browser on each. Chrome, on either device, is the safest default; Firefox and Edge also work.

Steps

  1. On the PC, go to dropitover.com.
  2. On the phone, open the same address in its browser.
  3. Within a second or two, each device shows up in the other's "Devices on your Wi-Fi" list.
  4. On whichever device has the file, tap the other device's name, choose the file (or several), and send.
  5. The receiving device gets a prompt to accept. Once accepted, the transfer starts immediately and streams straight from one browser to the other.

If the devices don't see each other: the most common cause is that one is on mobile data instead of Wi-Fi, or the router has "AP isolation" (also called "client isolation") turned on, which some public and office networks use to stop devices seeing each other on purpose. A home network almost never has this enabled.

Sending photos specifically

Select multiple photos at once from the file picker; there's no count limit on a same-network transfer. For a very large batch, zipping them first on the phone keeps it to one transfer instead of many, though it isn't required.

If they're not on the same Wi-Fi

This exact method needs both devices on one network. If the phone is on mobile data, or the PC is somewhere else entirely, use "Send a link" instead: upload once, get a link, open it on the PC whenever. It works across any two networks and doesn't need both sides online at the same moment.

Does it work the other way, PC to Android?

Yes, identically. Open the site on both, pick the PC from the list on the phone, and send.

How fast is it, really?

Because the file goes directly between the two devices over the local network rather than out to a server and back, transfer speed is mostly limited by your router and the two devices' Wi-Fi radios, not by an internet connection. On a typical home network, a few hundred megabytes moves in well under a minute. A weak Wi-Fi signal on either device is the most common cause of a slow transfer, more often than the file size itself.
