Understanding Local Area Network (LAN) Wireless Throughput

Modern home and office Wi-Fi routers (Wi-Fi 5 / 802.11ac, Wi-Fi 6 / 802.11ax, and Wi-Fi 7) are capable of theoretical local transfer speeds between 1,200 Mbps and 9,600 Mbps (150 MB/s to 1.2 GB/s).

Yet, most users experience agonizingly slow file transfer speeds because their tools route files out across the internet to a third-party server and back down, rather than utilizing the massive local wireless bandwidth already available inside their router.

Technical Factors That Dictate Wi-Fi Transfer Speed

Wi-Fi Standard / Frequency Channel Width Typical Real-World P2P Transfer Speed
Wi-Fi 6 / 6E (5 GHz / 6 GHz) 80 MHz – 160 MHz 80 – 120+ MB/s (Gigabit equivalent)
Wi-Fi 5 (5 GHz) 40 MHz – 80 MHz 45 – 75 MB/s
Legacy 2.4 GHz Band 20 MHz (Congested) 3 – 8 MB/s

How to Configure Your Environment for Maximum Wireless Speed

1
Switch Both Devices to the 5 GHz / 6 GHz Band

2.4 GHz Wi-Fi suffers from heavy interference from microwave ovens, Bluetooth accessories, and neighboring networks. Ensure both sending and receiving devices are connected to the 5 GHz network SSID.

2
Disable Router "AP / Client Isolation"

Some commercial and guest Wi-Fi routers enable "Access Point Isolation" to prevent devices from communicating directly. For seamless home and office P2P discovery, disable Client Isolation in your router's wireless security settings.

3
Connect One Device via Gigabit Ethernet (If Available)

If your desktop PC or laptop is connected to the router via a physical Cat6 Ethernet cable while your phone is on Wi-Fi, you eliminate half of the wireless airtime contention, effectively doubling your transfer throughput!

4
Open DropItOver in Modern Browsers

Navigate to dropitover.com. Both devices will pair over local WebRTC DataChannels and stream data directly across router antennas with zero compression.

Advanced Router Tuning: Channel Width, DFS Channels & QoS

To squeeze maximum gigabit performance out of your local network router:

  • Set Channel Width to 80 MHz or 160 MHz: Standard 20 MHz or 40 MHz settings limit throughput to ~200 Mbps. Switching to 80/160 MHz on 5 GHz allows 1,200+ Mbps physical link rates.
  • Select Uncongested DFS Channels: In dense apartment complexes, standard 5 GHz channels (36–48) are crowded. Enabling DFS channels (52–144) provides clean, interference-free spectrum.
  • Enable WMM (Wi-Fi Multimedia) QoS: Ensures high-priority queueing for real-time WebRTC UDP packets.

Troubleshooting Common Local Network Discovery Blockers

  • VPN Connections: Active VPN clients (such as NordVPN, ExpressVPN, or corporate WireGuard/OpenVPN tunnels) route all traffic through virtual adapters, hiding your device from local LAN discovery. Temporarily pause your VPN or enable "Allow LAN traffic" in your VPN settings.
  • Windows Defender / Third-Party Firewall: If your Windows network profile is set to "Public Network", Windows Firewall may restrict local port discovery. Change your network profile to "Private Network" in Windows Settings.
  • Mesh Wi-Fi Subnet Partitioning: If your home uses multiple mesh access points, ensure all nodes are configured in bridge mode so all devices receive IP addresses on the same /24 subnet.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my transfer slow down when transferring hundreds of small files?
Operating systems must create separate file handles and directory entries for every individual file. For maximum speed when transferring thousands of photos or code files, zip them into a single archive before sending.
Can I transfer files over Wi-Fi without an active internet connection?
WebRTC signaling requires a brief initial handshake to exchange connection parameters. Once the DataChannel is established, all binary file data flows purely across local Wi-Fi without using external internet data.
Does distance from the Wi-Fi router affect speed?
Yes. Wireless signal strength (RSSI) directly impacts Wi-Fi PHY modulation rates. Staying within 10–15 feet of your wireless access point delivers maximum throughput.
Why is Ethernet + Wi-Fi faster than Wi-Fi to Wi-Fi?
Wi-Fi is half-duplex. When both devices are wireless, the router must receive packets on the Wi-Fi radio and retransmit them on the same frequency, splitting airtime in half. Connecting one device via Ethernet allows simultaneous full-duplex transmission.
What browser provides the fastest WebRTC transfer speeds?
Chromium-based browsers (Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Brave) and Apple Safari offer highly optimized WebAssembly and hardware-accelerated crypto pipelines for peak transfer rates.
How do I know if my devices are using direct LAN sockets?
When devices are on the same local subnet, DropItOver shows 'Devices on your Wi-Fi'. Transfers initiate instantly with speeds matching local router hardware capacity.
