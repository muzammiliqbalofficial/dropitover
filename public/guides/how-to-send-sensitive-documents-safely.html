Practical engineering guide

Send sensitive documents with a clear threat model

Reviewed against the implementation: October 17, 2026

Before choosing a transfer button, decide who must be prevented from reading the document and what happens after the recipient saves it. A bank statement sent to a trusted adviser has different requirements from a public brochure, and an organizational policy may require an approved records system. DropItOver provides live browser transfers and temporary links, but neither mode verifies a recipient's real-world identity or prevents them from keeping a copy. This guide explains the actual boundaries and a practical verification workflow. It is technical guidance for using the tool, not a claim that a transfer automatically satisfies a particular regulatory regime.

Minimize the document and identify the recipient

Send only the pages or fields needed for the task. Review filenames, hidden comments, tracked changes, embedded metadata, and attachments before selecting the file. Redaction should remove underlying content rather than merely cover it visually. Create a non-sensitive test document if you need to check compatibility first. Confirm the recipient through an existing trusted channel, especially if someone unexpectedly requests records or changes their contact address. A familiar display nickname in a nearby list is not identity verification and can be imitated by another participant.

Ask whether the recipient can stay online and whether server-managed storage is permitted. If the answer is unclear, resolve that before uploading. Avoid sending passwords, private keys, recovery codes, or documents you are not authorized to disclose. For an organizational workflow, follow its approved retention and recipient-verification process. A tool offering a short expiry does not waive those requirements, and a successful encrypted connection does not prove that the selected recipient should have the file.

Understand the live transfer boundary

Nearby and room file payloads use SCTP over DTLS between browsers. TURN can relay the encrypted packets if direct ICE connectivity fails. The signaling service still processes connection metadata, participant names, transfer offers, SDP, and ICE candidates. A filename can itself reveal sensitive information before the bytes are sent, so choose neutral names where appropriate. Both browsers must remain active, and recipients can save copies. Browser-delivered code and the devices running it remain part of the trust model.

Live payload: Sender browser ===== DTLS-encrypted data ===== Recipient
Metadata: browsers ----- signaling service ----- browsers
Stored link: plaintext in Worker -> AES-GCM ciphertext in R2 -> Worker plaintext

A room URL grants an invitation to the room rather than a named-account permission check. Keep it within the intended group and confirm who joined. Public-IP discovery can also group unrelated people behind one shared gateway. Do not treat discovery as proof that someone is physically nearby or belongs to your organization. If recipient identity has serious consequences, verify it out of band and use a system with the identity and access controls your process requires.

Understand the stored-link boundary

Link uploads travel over HTTPS to a Worker, which processes plaintext and encrypts file chunks with AES-256-GCM before writing to R2. The Worker holds the master key and later decrypts downloads. This is encryption at rest controlled by the service, not zero-knowledge encryption with a key held only by the sender. D1 stores metadata and notes separately. Do not put secrets into a note or filename on the assumption that file encryption protects every field in the database.

If your threat model excludes the service operator, encrypt the document independently with a tool and format the recipient already supports before uploading. Exchange the decryption key through a separate, authenticated communication channel. Avoid sending the key beside the link in the same message when separation is the point of your control. Confirm that the recipient can decrypt a test archive first. A forgotten archive password is not recoverable by the file-transfer service, and a weak password can undermine otherwise strong encryption.

Choose retention deliberately

Select an expiry that leaves enough time for the intended recipient to download, while avoiding unnecessary availability. Links stop resolving after expiry and scheduled cleanup removes stored data on a ten-minute cycle under normal operation. Operational failures can delay physical deletion. Burn-after-read removes a share after the required items have been collected, and the owner can delete earlier using the private token. None of these operations erases a screenshot, forwarded copy, browser download, or independently retained record.

Keep the source under your own retention policy until the recipient confirms a usable copy. If the document must be a formal record, place it in the appropriate controlled system after transfer rather than using an expiring URL as the only record. Do not make promises of instantaneous physical overwriting or universal legal compliance based on a countdown timer. The Privacy Policy documents what this service stores, and support can handle applicable requests for support records without pretending that no-account use means no personal data exists.

Verify delivery without leaking diagnostics

Begin with a harmless test file on the intended devices and network. Keep tabs foregrounded, especially on iOS Safari, and avoid screen lock during live delivery. On Windows, review browser firewall permissions and the active network profile if connection setup fails; do not disable protection globally. On guest Wi-Fi, AP Isolation may block a direct path. A trusted hotspot can diagnose that restriction, but do not move a confidential workflow to an unapproved network merely because it is technically easier.

After receiving, compare size and open the saved copy. For important documents, compare SHA-256 digests through an authenticated channel when practical. A digest proves byte agreement with the selected source, not authorship or authorization. Inspect the actual pages and attachments so a correctly transmitted wrong document does not pass unnoticed. If something fails, retain the original and report the stage, browser versions, network arrangement, and approximate size using a non-sensitive reproduction. Never attach the private document just to prove that transfer is broken.

Plan time and memory without false assurance

Large scanned records or archives can expose mobile memory limits. WebRTC receives assemble Blob parts but retain the whole received object for saving; a small send chunk does not establish constant total memory. Test a representative harmless file and monitor resources. Stored links use different buffers and default limits of two GiB per file and eight GiB per bundle. Neither method is a malware scanner or an archival verification system.

Use the process that meets your recipient, access, retention, and device requirements, and stop if any of those requirements cannot be met. The encryption guide provides key and chunk details; the contact page accepts private support tickets, but it should not receive your passwords, confidential documents, or working download URLs as routine troubleshooting material.
