DropItOver vs AirDrop

AirDrop is fast and it works well, if both devices are made by Apple. This page is for the moment that stops being true: an Android phone, a Windows laptop, a Linux machine, anything that isn't in Apple's ecosystem.

Last checked 19 August 2026

The short version: AirDrop is a proximity feature built into iOS and macOS. It has no version for Windows, Android or Linux, and Apple has never announced plans to build one. If even one of your two devices isn't a Mac or an iPhone, AirDrop simply isn't an option, and nothing about that is likely to change.

When AirDrop is genuinely the better choice

If both devices are Apple hardware and they're within a few metres of each other, AirDrop is hard to beat. It's already installed, the handshake is instant, and there's no browser tab to open. For a photo passed between two iPhones at a table, use AirDrop.

When that stops being true

  • One device isn't Apple's. A Windows PC, an Android phone, a Chromebook, a Linux box: AirDrop can't see any of them, in either direction.
  • The other person isn't there yet. AirDrop needs both devices awake and in range at the same moment. If you want to leave something for someone to grab later, that's a link, which AirDrop doesn't have.
  • You're not on the same network, or not even in the same city. AirDrop is proximity-only. DropItOver's room mode connects two devices on entirely different networks, live.

How DropItOver does the "same Wi-Fi" case: open the site on both devices and they find each other automatically, the same way AirDrop does. Accept the transfer, and the file goes straight from one browser to the other. Nothing passes through a server.

The honest limitation

AirDrop's proximity handshake is genuinely faster to start for two Apple devices sitting next to each other, because there's no browser to open. DropItOver trades that half-second for working everywhere else AirDrop doesn't: other platforms, other networks, and other times.
